In Türkiye, November is the month when online stores carry a load unlike the rest of the year.
Traffic, orders and payment volume are squeezed into a few weeks, and an unprepared site slows down or
stops on the very day it gets the most visitors. In 2026, Black Friday falls on Friday 27 November,
which leaves roughly five weeks from today. This post uses Turkish data to show how large the
November load is, what needs to be prepared in advance for speed, infrastructure, payments, pricing
rules and shipping, and a week-by-week timeline. You can find a short summary of payment
infrastructure options [in our FAQ](/en/faq#faq-payment-integration).

## Why November is the hardest month of the year

According to the Ministry of Trade's [Outlook of E-Commerce in Türkiye 2025 report](https://www.alomaliye.com/2026/05/12/turkiyede-e-ticaretin-gorunumu-raporu-2025-yayimlandi/),
e-commerce volume in the November 2025 campaign period was 41.5% higher than in the same period a year
earlier, and the number of goods and services sold rose 11.6%. A single company's data shows the same
intensity: [according to the e-commerce logistics company fiCommerce](https://www.usakhabergazetesi.com.tr/kasim-ayi-e-ticarette-yuzde-20-ile-zirve-yapti),
November made up 20% of the year's total order volume, orders rose 45% compared with October, and
the busiest day was 28 November.

On the payments side, [iyzico's November 2025 data](https://mediacat.com/black-friday-ne-getirdi-ne-goturdu/)
shows that it processed 1,000 transactions per second on Friday 28 November, that transaction volume
rose 22% year on year, and that the average basket was 2,656 TL. According to Gemius, e-commerce
platforms had 52 million visitors in total between 1 and 15 November.

## The market has grown, and so have expectations

The market the campaign plays out in grows every year. [According to ETBİS data](https://bigpara.hurriyet.com.tr/haberler/ekonomi-haberleri/turkiyenin-e-ticaret-haritasi-guncellendi-e-ticarette-ikinci-el-ruzgari_ID100701633/),
total e-commerce volume rose from 1.85 trillion TL in 2023 to 4.57 trillion TL in 2025. The number of
businesses selling online reached 634,000, which means more competitors in November too.

Demand is widening as well. According to TurkStat's [Household ICT Usage Survey 2026](https://www.cnbce.com/haberler/turkiyede-internetten-alisveriste-rekor-her-10-kisiden-6si-online-siparis-veriyor-h34434),
the share of people ordering goods or services online rose from 55.7% in 2025 to 60% in 2026, and
internet use reached 92.3%. Many of the visitors who reach your site in November have already had a
fast checkout and fast delivery elsewhere; that experience sets the bar.

## Infrastructure: can it handle the traffic peak

Campaign traffic is planned for the peak, not the average. A global example shows the scale:
[Shopify reported](https://www.shopify.com/investors/press-releases/shopify-merchants-achieve-record-breaking-146-billion-black)
that its merchants together reached $5.1 million in sales per minute on Black Friday 2025, with the
peak at 12:01 PM US Eastern time. Load does not spread evenly across the day; it piles up at certain
moments. On your site, too, the riskiest moment is usually the first hour after the campaign email or
notification goes out.

Before the campaign, test the following:

- **Caching and CDN:** product listings, categories and campaign pages should be served from cache
  instead of hitting the database on every visit.
- **Load testing:** test with several times a normal day's traffic, especially on the cart and
  checkout steps. A home page that loads is not enough proof.
- **Third-party scripts:** extra scripts such as live chat, heatmaps and ad tags slow pages at the
  peak; remove the ones you do not need before the campaign.
- **Monitoring and alerts:** set alerts for error rate, payment success and page time, and decide in
  advance who responds when an alert fires.

<Callout type="warn" title="Freeze changes two weeks before the campaign">
  A new feature shipped the day before the campaign carries the risk of an untested bug into the
  busiest day. Freeze code and infrastructure changes two weeks before the campaign and allow only bug
  fixes during that time.
</Callout>

## Payments: do not rely on a single channel

ETBİS data shows that payment habits in Türkiye are distinctive. In 2025,
[62.5% of e-commerce payments were made by card and 29.2% by bank transfer or EFT](https://www.alomaliye.com/2026/05/12/turkiyede-e-ticaretin-gorunumu-raporu-2025-yayimlandi/);
cash on delivery accounted for 3.5%. 3D Secure was used in 64.1% of card payments. The share of bank
transfers rose within a year.

The practical takeaways:

- **A backup payment channel:** if the card infrastructure has a problem, bank transfer or a second
  virtual POS should be ready so the order is not lost.
- **Wallets and fast checkout:** in iyzico's November data, digital wallet payments
  [reached 30% of all transactions](https://mediacat.com/black-friday-ne-getirdi-ne-goturdu/); saved
  cards and wallets shorten the payment step.
- **Showing instalments:** instalment options should be clear on the product page; a difference
  first seen at checkout makes people abandon the cart.

## Prices and discounts: the 30-day rule

The legal side of a campaign is the part most often skipped. According to Türkiye's Ministry of
Trade, [the discount should be based on the lowest price applied in the 30 days before the discount starts](https://www.forbes.com.tr/ekonomi/ticaret-bakanligi-ndan-indirimli-satis-uyarisi),
and the previous price should be shown in the ad. The campaign's start and end dates, the quantity it
covers and any stock limit should be stated clearly and understandably; misleading wording or visuals
about discounted products and rates should not be used.

Enforcement is real: in the first half of 2025, the Advertising Board
[reviewed 12,499 files, found violations in 753 and imposed 141 million TL in fines](https://www.diken.com.tr/?p=1606389).

<Callout type="key" title="Keep price history in the system">
  The most reliable way to follow the 30-day rule is to store each product's price history in the
  system and calculate the "previous price" on the campaign label automatically from that record. An
  old price entered by hand leaves both room for error and a record that is hard to defend in an
  inspection.
</Callout>

## Shipping and the delivery promise

According to ETBİS data, average delivery time
[fell from 46.2 hours in 2023 to 42.2 hours in 2025](https://bigpara.hurriyet.com.tr/haberler/ekonomi-haberleri/turkiyenin-e-ticaret-haritasi-guncellendi-e-ticarette-ikinci-el-ruzgari_ID100701633/).
Customers get used to that speed, but carriers are also overloaded in November. The delivery time on
product pages should be updated for the campaign period, and stock and warehouse capacity should be
set against the order forecast. If you sell the same product on marketplaces and on your own site,
stock synchronisation stops the same item from being sold on both channels and one order being
cancelled.

| 3 weeks out | Campaign pages, discount labels based on price history, a payment fallback |

| Campaign week | Monitoring and bug fixes only; daily check-ins with support and shipping |

## Common mistakes

- **Launching the campaign page on the last day.** Search engines and ad approvals need time; a
  permanent campaign URL can be reused every year.
- **Testing only the home page.** What really stresses the system is the cart, the stock check and
  the payment step.
- **Depending on a single payment provider.** An outage at the peak is a direct loss of sales on a
  site with no backup.
- **Calculating the discount rate by hand.** Breaching the 30-day rule risks both fines and lost
  trust.
- **Running the campaign without measurement.** Spending without knowing which channel brought sales
  leaves next year's budget to guesswork.

## Conclusion

November is an exam for an online store. The data shows the load grows every year: campaign volume
rose 41.5%, and the share of people shopping online reached 60%. Most of the work needed to pass the
exam is technical but simple: improve speed, set up a payment fallback, record price history and
freeze changes on time. What makes the difference is having all of it done before the peak.
