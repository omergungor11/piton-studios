stores: Google's "up to two weeks" means the rollout may finish only a few days before Black Friday. This post
explains what the update is, how it compares with the other 2026 updates, what Google recommends to sites whose
rankings change, and what to do and not do before sales week. We briefly explain how long SEO results take
[on our FAQ page](/en/faq#faq-seo-timeline).

## What happened?

[According to the entry on the Google Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/incidents/WLxcJyC6jMWMfryxsKMc),
the update started on 10 November 2026 at 10:06 US Pacific time. Google's note is short: the November 2026 core
update was released, it applies globally and to all languages, and the rollout may take up to two weeks. As of
12 November, there is no completion note on the dashboard; the update still shows as "active".

[According to PPC Land](https://ppc.land/googles-core-update-shifts-rankings-for-up-to-2-weeks-before-black-friday/),
this is Google's third core update of 2026, and Google gave no indication of which categories would be affected or
why. Based on the two-week window, the article calculates the latest finish date as Tuesday 24 November, three days
before Black Friday on 27 November.

## What is a core update, and how is it different from a spam update?

[According to Google's core update documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates),
several times a year Google makes significant, broad changes to its search algorithms and systems. These changes
don't target specific sites or individual pages; the aim is for search results to be more helpful overall. When a
site's rankings drop, it doesn't mean the site broke a rule; it can mean other pages are now judged a better fit.

| | Core update | Spam update |
|---|---|---|
| What changes? | How the search systems assess content, broadly | The automated systems that detect spam |
| Target | Not specific sites | Sites violating the spam policies |
| If you're affected | Assess the whole site for helpful, reliable content | Review the spam policies |
| How many in 2026? | 3 (March, May, November) | 4 (March, June, August, September) |

We covered the September spam update and Google's new rules on AI content in
[our post on Google and AI content](/en/blog/google-ai-content-quality-rules).

## The 2026 calendar: how long did the updates take?

The history on Google's status dashboard gives each update's start date and duration. Both 2026 core updates
finished a little under Google's two-week upper limit: the March update took 12 days 4 hours and the May update
11 days 21 hours. The December 2025 update took 18 days 2 hours.

## Why does the overlap with Black Friday matter?

November is already a month when searches fluctuate in many sectors. [Google's traffic drop documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/debugging-search-traffic-drops)
also lists seasonality as one of the usual causes of drops. When a core update and a sales period overlap, it becomes
harder to tell which one caused a traffic change: a rise in a product category may come from seasonal demand and a
drop from the update, or the other way round.

We covered how to get your online store ready for sales week in [our November sales readiness post](/en/blog/november-sales-ecommerce-readiness-guide).
The core update adds one more item to that list: big changes made while the update is rolling out make it impossible
to measure their effect, and put sales week at risk.

<Callout type="warn" title="Don't make big changes while the update is rolling out">
  Google recommends waiting for the update to finish before assessing whether you were affected. Changing the URL
  structure, merging category pages or migrating the site during the rollout mixes up the effect of the update with
  the effect of your own change. Right before Black Friday, that risk doubles.
</Callout>

## How do you know if you were affected?

The order in [Google's documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates) is clear.
First, confirm on the Search Status Dashboard that the update has finished. Then wait at least a full week after it
completes before analysing your site in Search Console. Then compare the periods before and after the update, and
look at which pages, queries and countries the drop is in.

Google splits drops into two groups:

| Type of drop | Google's advice |
|---|---|
| Small drop | No need for drastic action; avoid radical changes on pages that perform well |
| Large, sustained drop | Assess whether the site as a whole, not individual pages, delivers helpful, reliable, people-first content |

[The traffic drop documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/debugging-search-traffic-drops)
points the same way: changes to a site take time to show an effect, and in general you'll want to wait a few weeks
before analysing your site in Search Console again. The update isn't always the cause either; the same documentation
lists technical issues, security issues, spam issues, seasonality and site moves as separate causes.

## What to do if the drop is large

For large, sustained drops, Google recommends assessing the whole site with the question list in its
[helpful content documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content): is
the content original, written by someone who knows the subject, satisfying for the reader, and made for people rather
than for ranking well in search? The documentation advises avoiding "quick fix" changes.

Google is candid about recovery time: some changes can take effect in a few days, but it could take several months.
If several months have passed with no effect, you may need to wait for the next core update. But you don't always
have to wait for a big update, because Google also makes smaller, unannounced core updates. And Google states plainly
that there is no guarantee changes to your site will have a noticeable impact in search results.

<Callout type="tip" title="Document the drop">
  Export and keep the Search Console data for the four weeks before the update started (by page, query, country and
  device). When you analyse after the one-week wait, you'll have a clean baseline to compare against, instead of
  figures mixed with sales-period data.
</Callout>

## Checklist

| When? | What to do |
|---|---|
| Now, during the rollout | Export current data; postpone big structural changes |
| Before Black Friday | Focus on sales-critical work such as speed, checkout and stock; don't change URLs or categories |
| When the update finishes | Check for the completion note on the Search Status Dashboard |
| At least 1 week after it finishes | Compare before and after periods in Search Console by page and query |
| If the drop is small | Keep monitoring, make no radical changes |
| If the drop is large and sustained | Assess the whole site with Google's question list and plan improvements |
| The following months | Measure the effect every few weeks; be patient |

## Common mistakes

- **Drawing conclusions during the rollout.** Rankings can fluctuate throughout; Google recommends waiting for it to
  finish and then another week.
- **Blaming every drop on the core update.** In November, seasonality, campaigns and technical issues also change
  traffic.
- **Trying to fix a single page.** For large drops, Google recommends looking at the whole site.
- **Looking for a quick fix.** Rewriting titles in bulk or deleting content are the kind of changes Google doesn't
  recommend.
- **Migrating the site before Black Friday.** Leave a redesign or platform move until after sales week; we covered
  the SEO side of a migration in [our website redesign post](/en/blog/website-redesign-seo-migration).
- **Assuming recovery is guaranteed.** Google explicitly doesn't guarantee it.

## Conclusion

The November 2026 core update lands very close to the busiest sales week of the year. Google's advice is the same as
always: wait for the update to finish, let another week pass, then look at the data; stay calm on small drops and
assess the whole site on large ones. The smartest thing to do before sales week is to postpone big changes and focus
on the work that matters for sales.
