For a long time, web accessibility in Türkiye sat under "nice to have". Presidential Circular No. 2025/10,
published in June 2025, changed that: alongside public bodies, banks, private hospitals, private
schools, transport companies, large operators and e-commerce sites are required to make their digital
services accessible to people with disabilities. The one-year period given to most organisations ended
in June 2026; the two-year period for e-commerce ends in June 2027. This post covers who the circular
applies to, how it is monitored, where websites stand today and where to start in the time left. A
short answer on what accessibility means is on our [FAQ page](/en/faq#faq-accessibility).

## What does the circular say?

According to [Erdem & Erdem's summary](https://www.erdem-erdem.av.tr/bilgi-bankasi/web-siteleri-ve-mobil-uygulamalarin-erisilebilirligi-hakkinda-2025-10-sayili-cumhurbaskanligi-genelgesi-yayimlandi),
the circular was published in Official Gazette No. 32933 on 21 June 2025. Websites and mobile
applications are assessed against two references: the "Accessibility Checklist for Websites and Mobile
Applications – Level A" published by the Ministry of Family and Social Services, and W3C's WCAG 2.2.

What makes the circular significant is that it is not limited to the public sector.
[KST Law's analysis on Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/turkey/media-telecoms-it-entertainment/monitoring-framework-for-digital-accessibility-standards-announced-for-t%C3%BCrkiye)
lists the organisations covered and their deadlines in detail:

| Organisation type | Deadline (from publication) |
|---|---|
| Public bodies, universities, municipalities and their companies, public economic enterprises | 1 year |
| Professional organisations with public institution status | 1 year |
| Banks and private hospitals | 1 year |
| Private schools opened with Ministry of National Education permission | 1 year |
| Road, rail, air and sea passenger transport companies, Type A travel agencies | 1 year |
| Electronic communications companies with over 200,000 subscribers | 1 year |
| E-commerce service providers under Law No. 6563 | 2 years |

Deadlines run from the publication date. By our calculation, the one-year period ended on 21 June 2026
and the two-year period ends on 21 June 2027. For an e-commerce site, that leaves roughly eight months.

## Who monitors it, and how?

The circular sets up a monitoring structure. As Erdem & Erdem reports, there is a Monitoring Commission
chaired by the Minister of Family and Social Services and an Advisory Commission chaired by a deputy
minister, with public and civil society representatives. Each organisation in scope also forms its own
review commission, which technically reviews its websites and apps and reports the results to the
Monitoring Commission.

According to the Legal 500 analysis, the Monitoring Commission decides which websites and apps are
audited through an annual monitoring plan. Organisations found accessible earn the right to display
an "Accessibility Logo" on their digital platforms for two years.

<Callout type="info" title="No direct fine is defined">
  The sources we reviewed do not mention a fine tied to the circular. [A commentary on Hukuki Haber](https://www.hukukihaber.net/erisilebilirlik-artik-bir-zorunluluk-kamu-web-siteleri-ve-mobil-uygulamalara-yeni-duzenleme)
  notes that, even without a direct sanction, compliance may later be used as a criterion in audits
  and public spending decisions. The absence of a fine is a chance to plan the work, not to postpone it.
</Callout>

## Where do websites stand today?

No comprehensive Türkiye-specific scan has been published; the most reliable general picture is
WebAIM's yearly automated scan of the top 1 million home pages. [The 2026 report](https://webaim.org/projects/million/)
detected at least one WCAG failure on 95.9% of home pages. The rate was 97.8% in 2019 and improved in
small steps for six years to 94.8% in 2025; in 2026 that trend reversed.

The number of errors per page rose too. The average went from 51 in 2025 to 56.1 in 2026, an increase
of 10.1%. [The 2024 report](https://webaim.org/projects/million/2024) gives 56.8 for 2024 and 50 for
2023; the picture is fluctuating, not improving.

## Most errors come from six issues

The good news is that the errors are not scattered. According to WebAIM, the six error types below
account for 96% of all errors detected. The most common is low-contrast text, found on 83.9% of home
pages in 2026.

The eight-year series shows which problems got fixed and which persist. Missing document language fell
from 33.1% to 13.5%; it is a one-line fix, so it dropped quickly. Missing alternative text fell from
68% to 53.1%. Low contrast, however, has barely moved, and missing form labels have been rising again
since 2023.

Each of these six errors directly affects the path to purchase on an e-commerce site: an unreadable
price, a product photo the screen reader announces only as "image", a search box with no name, an
add-to-cart icon read out as just "button". For the form side, see our
[accessible form design guide](/en/blog/accessible-web-design-forms).

## Where to start in the time left

For e-commerce sites, the work that can be done before June 2027 is, with the right order, a fix plan
rather than a redesign project:

| Step | What to do | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Scope | List the home, category, product, cart, checkout, account and contact flows | List of pages and flows to audit |
| 2. Automated scan | Scan these pages with an accessibility tool | Errors grouped by type |
| 3. Quick fixes | Document language, alternative text, accessible names for empty links and buttons | Most errors closed |
| 4. Design fixes | Contrast in the colour palette, visible focus ring, adequate touch targets | Updated design system |
| 5. Task tests | Finding a product, adding to cart and checking out with keyboard and screen reader | Results per flow |
| 6. Process | A checklist for new content and campaign pages | Errors do not come back |

<Callout type="tip" title="Fix shared components first">
  Most errors are not on individual pages but in components repeated on every page: the main menu,
  product cards, filters and forms. Fixing one component removes the same error from hundreds of
  pages at once. If you use a theme or an off-the-shelf platform, this is also the stage where it
  becomes clear whether a problem comes from the theme or from your own additions.
</Callout>

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming an accessibility plugin makes you compliant.** A toolbar added to the page offers contrast
  or text size buttons; it does not fix missing labels, a menu that cannot be used with a keyboard or a
  button with no name.
- **Relying only on automated scans.** A scan finds structural errors; it does not show whether checkout
  can be completed with a keyboard.
- **Fixing only the home page.** What gets audited, and where users get things done, is usually the
  product, cart and checkout pages.
- **Forgetting the mobile app.** The circular covers mobile applications as well as websites.
- **Doing it once and stopping.** Every new campaign page, banner or product image can bring old errors
  back; the checklist has to be part of the content process.

## Conclusion

The circular turned accessibility in Türkiye into a time-bound obligation for a broad group of private
organisations for the first time. For most of them, the deadline has passed; e-commerce sites have
until June 2027. WebAIM's data shows both how widespread the problem is and where to start: 96% of
errors come from six recurring issues, and most of them are fixed in shared components.
