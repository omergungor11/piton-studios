A customer saying "my budget is €70, find me winter running shoes and add the best one to my cart", and an AI
assistant doing the rest, is no longer an experiment. In 2026 Google, OpenAI and large retailers started scaling this
way of shopping, known as **agentic commerce**, with real payments. In this post we cover what changed, what the first
data says, where Türkiye stands and what an online store can prepare for now. If you are curious about the conversion
side, see the [related question on our FAQ page](/en/faq#faq-conversion-optimization).

## What does an AI shopping agent do?

In classic search the user clicks results, browses sites and decides alone. With a shopping agent, the assistant takes
over some of those steps: it researches the product, compares options, summarises reviews, fills the cart and starts
the payment once the user approves. The sale still goes through the store;
[according to Google's Universal Cart announcement](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/shopping/universal-cart-launch/),
the merchant remains the seller of record.

Control doesn't pass entirely to the agent.
[According to Modern Retail's 19 November roundup](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/in-graphic-detail-welcome-to-the-agentic-commerce-quarter/),
Walmart lets customers cap an agent's spending at $50, $100 or $200. Agentic shopping today mostly works as "the agent
prepares, the person approves".

## What happened in 2026?

The changes came piece by piece over the year rather than in one announcement. The table lists the source for each
row.

| Date | Development | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 11 Jan 2026 | Google announced the open shopping protocol UCP; checkout in AI Mode and Gemini for US retailers | Google |
| 24 Mar 2026 | OpenAI stepped back from the first in-ChatGPT checkout; purchases complete on the merchant's own checkout | OpenAI |
| 15 Oct 2026 | Google Universal Cart launched in the US: products from different stores in one cart | Google |
| 4 Nov 2026 | Google's agentic checkout began rolling out in Canada | Google Canada |
| 17 Nov 2026 | Merchant Center integration guide: gradual in the US, Australia and Canada in early 2027 | Google for Developers |
| Nov 2026 | OpenAI introduced ChatGPT Wallet | Modern Retail |

[According to Google's January announcement](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/agentic-commerce-ai-tools-protocol-retailers-platforms/),
the protocol was co-developed with Shopify, Etsy, Wayfair, Target and Walmart and is endorsed by more than 20 companies
including Adyen, Mastercard, Stripe and Visa. Google said in the same post that it planned to expand the feature
globally "in the coming months"; as of November, the markets that have opened are the US and Canada.

OpenAI is taking a more cautious path.
[According to OpenAI's 24 March post](https://openai.com/index/powering-product-discovery-in-chatgpt/), the first
checkout version "did not offer the level of flexibility" it wanted; the company now lets merchants use their own
checkout and focuses on product discovery. Merchants share product feeds and promotions through OpenAI's commerce
protocol, and Shopify stores' product data is already connected to ChatGPT through Shopify Catalog, so according to
OpenAI those merchants don't need to do anything extra. We covered the advertising side of ChatGPT separately in our
[ChatGPT ads post](/en/blog/chatgpt-ads-guide-for-businesses-in-turkiye).

## How valuable is a visitor from AI?

The first measurements show this traffic is small but valuable.
[According to Adobe's 29 October report](https://business.adobe.com/blog/ai-traffic-higher-revenue-per-visit-retail-holiday),
traffic from AI tools to US retail sites grew 71% year over year in September 2026, and these visitors converted 90%
better than other channels. Adobe's data covers more than 1 trillion visits to US retail sites. The same traffic
converted 9% worse in August 2025, so the picture reversed within a year.

## Are shoppers handing payment to agents?

There is a big gap between research and payment. In First Page Sage's US panel of 5,212 people, 62.3% of online
shoppers used AI to research products in October 2026, while only 7.9% let an agent complete checkout with their
approval. Fully autonomous shopping without approval was 1.5%.

agent is strongest before the decision: it shapes which store and which product gets recommended.

## Where does Türkiye stand?

At the time of writing, Google's agentic checkout is not live in Türkiye and no date has been announced.
[According to Google's Merchant Center guide](https://developers.google.com/merchant/ucp/guides/merchant-center/integration),
the integration is rolling out gradually in the US, with Australia and Canada planned for early 2027.
[Google Canada's 4 November announcement](https://blog.google/intl/en-ca/products/explore-get-answers/a-new-way-to-shop-introducing-a-more-intuitive-agentic-shopping-experience-for-consumers-and-retailers-in-canada/)
covers only Canadian retailers.

That splits stores in Türkiye into two groups. Exporters selling to the US or Canada can already consider joining these
channels. For stores that sell only domestically, checkout integration isn't relevant yet, but the product discovery
and comparison side of agents already works. Google's AI Mode and AI Overviews have been live in Türkiye since
February 2026; we covered that in our
[Google AI search controls post](/en/blog/google-ai-search-controls-ai-overviews-ai-mode).

<Callout type="info" title="Preparation starts before the checkout button">
  What a store needs to join agentic checkout when it reaches your country is useful today too: clean product data,
  correct prices and stock, clear return terms. These affect whether your store gets recommended in both classic
  search and AI answers.
</Callout>

## How do agents read a store?

An agent doesn't browse your product page like a person; it reads structured data. On Google's side, the hub for that
data is Merchant Center. According to Google's guide, native checkout requires a Merchant Center account, a shopping
profile published on the site, three interfaces for creating, updating and completing orders, and notifications that
report order status. For the "transfer to the merchant's cart" option alone, product IDs in Merchant Center must match
those on the site. Google's help page says Merchant Center will alert you when something is missing in the product
feed, return terms or account settings.

Marketers are preparing for this shift too. According to Gartner's 2026 survey cited by Modern Retail, 61% of senior
marketing leaders adapted their content and SEO for machine interpretability, and 39% tailored product information,
pricing or offers for agent decisions. For a store, that means a machine must be able to understand the product as well
as a person does.

## What you can do now

The steps below don't depend on the country; even before agentic checkout reaches Türkiye, they help visibility in
search and AI answers.

| Step | What to do | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product feed | Make the Merchant Center feed complete and error-free | The main data source for agents and Google's shopping surfaces |
| Product IDs | Match the IDs in the feed with those on the site | An explicit Google requirement for cart transfer |
| Price and stock | Make sure the price is the same on the site, in the feed and in the cart | If an agent builds a cart at the wrong price, the order and the trust are lost |
| Return terms | Write clear, consistent return and shipping terms | Merchant Center flags gaps in these fields |
| Structured data | Mark up product, price, stock and review data on product pages | So machines read the page correctly |
| Measurement | Track traffic from AI tools as a separate channel in analytics | To see its value in your own data |
| Infrastructure | Build ordering and payment with interfaces other systems can connect to | To join quickly when integration opens |

Price consistency is also a legal matter in Türkiye: since 1 August 2026, discount ads use the lowest price of the last
10 days as the reference, and there are new rules for reviews. Details are in our
[e-commerce review and discount rules post](/en/blog/turkiye-ecommerce-review-discount-ad-rules).

<Callout type="warn" title="Think before investing in an integration that isn't open yet">
  OpenAI stepping back from its first checkout version within months shows that this area is still changing fast.
  Instead of budgeting for a platform-specific integration that isn't available in Türkiye, strengthening the common
  foundation every platform needs, product data and a flexible ordering setup, is the safer investment.
</Callout>

## Common mistakes

- **Treating agentic commerce as just a checkout button.** Today agents have the most influence on product research
  and comparison; payment is the last link in the chain.
- **Setting up the product feed once and forgetting it.** A feed with outdated prices or stock leads an agent to
  recommend the wrong product, or not to recommend the store at all.
- **Applying US data directly to Türkiye.** The 90% conversion difference is a US measurement; see the result in your
  own store with your own analytics.
- **Not measuring AI traffic.** If this channel isn't tracked separately, its value disappears inside other channels.
- **Tying yourself to temporary platform-specific workarounds.** Rules and country lists keep changing; a flexible
  setup is less risky than depending on a single platform.

## Conclusion

AI shopping agents moved past the trial stage in 2026: Google opened checkout in the US and Canada, ChatGPT expanded
product discovery, and the first data shows visitors from this channel are valuable. In Türkiye checkout isn't live
yet, but the product discovery and comparison side of agents already works. The right move today is to make product
data, price consistency and measurement solid; when checkout integration arrives, the stores with this foundation in
place will get ahead.
