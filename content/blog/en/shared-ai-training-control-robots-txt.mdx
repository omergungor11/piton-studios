A website's content is read today for at least three different jobs: the search index, training AI models
and being cited as a source in AI answers. Until now, a site owner who wanted to stop training had to know
each company's bot and list them one by one in robots.txt. On 30 October 2026, Google, Microsoft and other
major providers announced they would support a single shared signal instead. This post explains how the
signal works, what it covers and what it doesn't, what the data says about the scale of AI crawling, and how
to make a decision for your site. We briefly explain what visibility in AI answers means on our
[FAQ page](/en/faq#faq-llm-visibility).

## What was announced?

[According to Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ai-training-control-42430.html),
Google announced it would voluntarily adopt the training preference proposal developed in the IETF's AI
Preferences (AIPREF) working group. In Google's words, it will help websites "express their AI training
preferences across multiple AI providers in a single step".

The technical text was published [as an individual IETF draft](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-kelley-aipref-training-preference-signal/).
Its author is Kevin Kelley of Anthropic, and the acknowledgements include people from Google, OpenAI,
Microsoft and Meta. The draft is not yet a standard adopted by the IETF, but the joint commitment of the major
providers turns it into a rule that will be applied in practice.

| Provider | Support status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Anthropic | Has started supporting it | Engadget |
| OpenAI | In the coming months | Engadget |
| Google | Early 2027 | Search Engine Roundtable |
| Microsoft | 1 February 2027 | Microsoft Bing Blog |
| Meta | Expressed support, has not confirmed it will honour it | iTech Post |

## How does the signal work?

The draft introduces no new file and no new grammar. It uses robots.txt's familiar user-agent group; the
difference is that the group is named after a purpose of use rather than a bot. The example in
[Microsoft's announcement](https://blogs.bing.com/search/2026/10/your-content-your-choice-clearer-preferences-for-training-and-grounding/)
and the draft looks like this:

```txt
# No AI training for the whole site, except the press kit
User-Agent: __usage_ai-training
Disallow: /
Allow: /press-kit/
```

The draft has a few important rules. The general group written for all bots (the asterisk line) does not
stand in for this signal; you have to write the group explicitly to state your preference. A rule for a
specific path takes precedence over a general one. Most importantly, the draft says the token "is not a
directive for crawling behavior": it concerns whether crawled content is used for training, not whether
bots crawl your site.

## Is Search affected?

No, and that distinction is at the centre of the decision. [According to Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2303225/google-openai-anthropic-ai-crawling-controls/),
the new group does not stop crawling altogether and does not prevent content from showing up in Google
Search. Google will interpret the signal on the training side as blocking Google-Extended, which collects
content for Gemini models. According to John Mueller of Google, as quoted by Search Engine Roundtable, this
applies when Google-Extended is not explicitly specified, and existing company-specific controls continue to work.

The controls for the three uses currently break down like this:

| Use | Affected by the shared training signal? | Separate control |
|---|---|---|
| Search index and results | No | noindex, search bot rules in robots.txt |
| AI model training | Yes | Company-specific bots (Google-Extended, GPTBot, ClaudeBot) keep working too |
| Being cited in AI answers | Not directly | Google: the generative AI control in Search Console; Bing: page-level grounding preference |

We covered Google's separate control for AI Overviews and AI Mode in search results in our
[post on Google's new AI control](/en/blog/google-ai-search-controls-ai-overviews-ai-mode).

<Callout type="warn" title="It doesn't undo past crawling, and it isn't a licence">
  The signal is only a statement of preference for future use. According to Engadget it does not undo
  content collected in the past, and according to iTech Post it does not create a licence, payment or
  copyright permission. If you want a contract for commercial use of your content, that is a separate matter.
</Callout>

## Microsoft draws a finer line

Microsoft's announcement brings separate page-level preferences for training and for citing sources while
generating answers (grounding). Grounding has three options: allow by default, allow only in Microsoft's own
products, and block entirely. Microsoft says these new preferences replace the old NoArchive and NoCache tags,
and that after 1 March 2027 those tags will no longer be read as training or grounding preferences. For sites
using these tags today, a temporary transition setting opens in Bing Webmaster Tools on 15 December 2026.

## What does the data say?

What makes the decision meaningful is the scale of AI crawling and what it gives back.
[According to Cloudflare's network data](https://blog.cloudflare.com/crawlers-click-ai-bots-training/),
over the past 12 months 80% of AI crawling was for training, 18% for search and only 2% for user actions.
Training's share rose from 72% in July 2024 to 79% in July 2025.

Site owners are already reacting. [According to the Consent in Crisis study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14933),
in a single year between 2023 and 2024 more than 28% of the most actively maintained, critical sources in
the widely used C4 training dataset were fully restricted from AI use through robots.txt. The shared signal
makes that decision easier without having to track each company's bot separately.

## A decision guide for your site

| Situation | Training | Search | Cited in AI answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corporate brochure site, service pages | Leaving it open usually makes sense | Open | Open |
| Blog, guides, site producing original content | Decide deliberately | Open | Open |
| Paid content, reports, courses | Consider closing it | Partly | Depends |
| Product catalogue and e-commerce | Usually open | Open | Open |
| Press kit, logos, official statements | Open | Open | Open |

For promotional and sales content, visibility is usually worth more than blocking: AI models knowing your
brand and services correctly is part of appearing in their answers. For original research, paid content or
labour-intensive guides, closing training is a legitimate choice.

<Callout type="tip" title="Inventory first, then the rule">
  Before adding a rule to robots.txt, list which content types your site has and which paths they sit under.
  Because the shared signal works per path, closing the blog while leaving the press kit open is only
  possible if content is organised into clean folders.
</Callout>

## Common mistakes

- **Closing search while closing training.** A rule that blocks the search bot can remove your site from
  search results. The shared signal is a separate group; leave your search bot rules alone.
- **Assuming the general rule is enough.** According to the draft, the general group for all bots does not
  stand in for this signal; you have to write the group explicitly.
- **Not reviewing company-specific rules.** Rules you wrote for Google-Extended, GPTBot or ClaudeBot keep
  working. If you wrote a separate rule for Google-Extended, Google does not replace it with the shared
  signal; make sure the two rules don't contradict each other.
- **Assuming support starts immediately.** Google and Microsoft start in 2027; until then the existing
  controls apply.
- **Deciding once and forgetting.** As new providers and new controls arrive, the robots.txt file should be
  reviewed at least once a year.

## Conclusion

The shared training signal delivers something site owners have wanted for years: deciding on AI training in
one place, with one rule. But the signal only concerns training; search and being cited in AI answers are
still managed separately. The right decision depends on the content type, and although the technical part is
one line, its consequences directly affect your visibility.
