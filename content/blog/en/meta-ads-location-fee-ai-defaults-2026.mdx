Meta ads are one of the most widely used digital ad channels for businesses in Türkiye. In the second half of 2026,
several changes arrived that advertisers may not notice without looking at their account: a location fee was added to
ads shown to audiences in Türkiye, some AI-powered features started switching on by default, and Meta prepared new
terms that make the advertiser responsible for content produced with these features. This post covers what changed,
which parts Meta has confirmed and what you should check in your account.

## What changed?

The changes were announced on different dates and through different channels; some are official Meta statements,
others are trade press reports of notices sent to advertisers. The table below gives the source for each row.

## The 5% location fee: Türkiye is on the list

[According to Reuters on 10 March 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/meta-charge-advertisers-fee-offset-182506077.html),
from 1 July Meta charges a location fee for ads shown in six countries: 2% in the UK; 3% in France, Italy and Spain;
and 5% in Austria and Türkiye. The fee covers image and video ads delivered on Meta platforms, click-to-WhatsApp
campaigns and marketing messages sent together with ads. Meta says it had covered additional costs such as digital
services taxes in these countries until now.

The key detail is that the fee depends on where the audience is, not on the advertiser's company address. A business
advertising to Türkiye from an account abroad and a Turkish business running a domestic campaign pay the same 5%.
As a simple calculation, a business spending €1,000 a month on audiences in Türkiye pays about €50 extra; this is a
worked example, and the real amount depends on the spend on your invoice.

<Callout type="warn" title="Check budgets and reports separately">
  Verify how the fee appears on invoices and in reports using Meta's help page and your own invoices. If you compare
  costs before and after July without accounting for it, a campaign can look worse than it is.
</Callout>

Because click-to-WhatsApp campaigns are included, businesses that sell over WhatsApp can see costs rise on two fronts.

## AI features that switch on by default

[According to Meta's notice as reported by PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-will-turn-on-product-browsing-in-existing-ads-on-october-28/),
from 28 October 2026 Meta adds product browsing to existing ads: people who see the ad can browse products without
leaving the app. Meta says a test with about 117,700 advertisers between 6 and 21 August showed 3% more conversions on
average. The feature can be turned off per ad in the Advantage+ creative and destination settings. According to PPC
Land, Meta published no separate announcement; the information comes from the notice sent to advertisers.

[According to AdMakeAI's roundup](https://admakeai.com/blog/meta-ads-updates-november-2026), the "measure
conversions on customer devices" setting in Events Manager is also on by default, with measurement starting on
12 November 2026; the EU and UK are excluded. Türkiye is not in that exception, so check whether the setting is on for
pixels used for Turkish audiences.

Control over placements is shrinking too.
[According to PPC Land in August](https://ppc.land/some-meta-advertisers-lose-placement-controls-with-bid-cuts-capped-at-90/),
some advertisers reported the placement option disappearing from Sales and Leads ad sets; the value rules that can be
used instead cut bids by at most 90%, so a placement can't be excluded completely. According to the same report,
Meta's Advantage+ placements page says ad sets using Advantage+ placements delivered an 11.7% lower cost per action on
average in an experiment; Meta did not publish the sample or dates.

<Callout type="tip" title="Test Meta's numbers against your own data">
  3% more conversions or 11.7% lower cost are averages from Meta's own experiments. Results in your account may
  differ. Before leaving a feature on, compare it where possible with the feature on in one campaign and off in a
  similar one, and verify the result with your site analytics.
</Callout>

We saw a similar trend at Google: we covered Google Ads automatically moving Search campaigns to AI Max in our
[AI Max migration post](/en/blog/google-ads-ai-max-migration-guide). The direction is the same on both platforms:
automation is the default, and control depends on the advertiser asking for it.

## New terms and responsibility

[According to The Keyword](https://www.thekeyword.co/news/meta-commercial-terms-ai-liability), the new section planned
for Meta's commercial terms makes the advertiser "solely responsible" for AI features and the content they produce,
bans altering AI watermarks and content-authentication data, and states that AI features may make mistakes. A Meta
spokesperson said advertisers can turn off individual Advantage+ features in Ads Manager.

The start date was first moved from 30 October to 20 November.
[According to Madgicx's review of 12 November](https://madgicx.com/blog/meta-ad-terms-update), Meta paused the updates
to its commercial terms, product catalogue terms, self-serve ad terms and generative AI ad terms; the existing terms
stay in effect until further notice, and no new date was given. We could not open Meta's own notice for this post;
check the current status in the Business Help Center notice.

Even with the terms paused, the message is clear: responsibility for ad content changed or generated with AI stays
with the advertiser. In Türkiye, since 1 August 2026, AI-generated characters that cannot be told apart from real
people must also be clearly disclosed in ads; we covered the details in our
[post on Türkiye's e-commerce review and discount rules](/en/blog/turkiye-ecommerce-review-discount-ad-rules).

These changes come down to two things. The first is cost: the location fee and rising ad prices can mean fewer
impressions for the same budget. The second is control: features such as product browsing, on-device measurement and
automatic placements switch on without you doing anything; where and how the ad appears, and how results are measured,
now depend more on Meta's system.

| Area | What changed | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Budget | 5% location fee for audiences in Türkiye | Recalculate your target cost per acquisition including the fee |
| Ad experience | Product browsing added to existing ads | Check the setting on each ad; choose what protects your store experience |
| Measurement | On-device conversion measurement on by default | Check the status in Events Manager and assess it against your data policy |
| Placements | Placement option removed from some ad sets | Watch placement reports and use value rules for unwanted placements |
| Content | Responsibility for AI content stays with the advertiser | Review AI-altered copy and images before they go live |

## Checklist

- **Compare invoices:** see how the location fee appears on invoices before and after July, and assess costs in
  reports including the fee.
- **Review Advantage+ creative settings:** check which enhancements are on for each ad, especially text variations,
  image edits and product browsing.
- **Check Events Manager:** see whether on-device conversion measurement is on.
- **Watch the placement report:** track weekly which placements the budget goes to.
- **Set up independent measurement:** compare conversions reported by Meta with site analytics and your order system.
- **Follow the terms:** watch for a new date for the paused terms update in the Business Help Center.

## Common mistakes

- **Mistaking the location fee for a performance drop.** Part of the cost increase after July comes from the fee;
  separate it out before changing campaign settings.
- **Never touching the default features.** A feature that should be off and one that could help should not both be
  left on the default setting; each should be a deliberate decision.
- **Relying on Meta's report alone.** The platform's own measurement isn't enough on its own to judge the effect of
  its own features.
- **Publishing AI output without review.** The advertiser is responsible for automatically generated headlines,
  translations or images; a wrong price or claim puts both the ad and the brand at risk.
- **Making big setting changes in Black Friday week.** Settle settings before the sales period starts; a big change
  while a campaign is running makes it harder to tell why results changed.

## Conclusion

Two trends became clear in Meta ads in 2026: for audiences in Türkiye, costs rose with a 5% location fee, and AI
features started switching on by default. The new terms that make advertisers responsible for AI content are paused
for now, but the direction hasn't changed. In this environment the most important job is knowing which features are
on and verifying results with independent measurement.
