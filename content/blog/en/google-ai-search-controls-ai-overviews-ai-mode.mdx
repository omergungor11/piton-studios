Since 18 February 2026, people searching Google in Türkiye see an AI summary above the results for
many questions: AI Overviews and the conversational AI Mode now work in Turkish. At the end of August
Google went a step further and, for the first time, let site owners **choose whether to appear in
these features at all**, along with a dedicated performance report. We gave the short answer to what
GEO is [in our FAQ](/en/faq#faq-what-is-geo); this post explains exactly what the new control does,
what the data says about clicks, and which setting each kind of business should pick.

## What changed in Türkiye

In its [announcement of 18 February 2026](https://blog.google/intl/tr-tr/urun-duyurulari/turkiyede-ai-modu-ve-ai-bakisi-donemi-basliyor/),
Google said search in Türkiye was moving to two new experiences powered by the Gemini 3 model family.
**AI Overviews** shows a summary answer with source links at the top of the results page. **AI Mode**
is a separate tab for long, multi-step questions asked as a conversation. Both rolled out gradually
and are available in the Android and iOS apps and on the web.

The scale is not small. According to Google's [announcement for website owners](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/new-controls-website-owners/),
AI Overviews reaches more than 2.5 billion monthly active users and AI Mode more than 1 billion monthly users.

## What happens to clicks: the data

The two most cited independent studies on how AI summaries affect clicks point the same way.
[Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)
analysed real browsing data from 900 US adults in March 2025. On pages with an AI summary, users
clicked a result on 8% of visits; on pages without one, the figure was 15%. Clicks on a link inside
the summary itself happened on just 1% of visits.

[Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/) compared Search Console data for
300,000 keywords between December 2023 and December 2025. For keywords that trigger an AI summary,
the desktop click-through rate of the top-ranking page fell from 7.3% to 1.6%. Informational keywords
without a summary declined too, but far less: from 7.6% to 3.9%.

## The new control in Search Console: three options

The control lives in Search Console under **Settings → Search generative AI**. Google announced it in
June, first opened it to a group of sites in the UK, and [as of 31 August 2026](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024?hl=en)
made it available to every website worldwide.

| Option | What happens | When it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Include my site's links and content (default) | Your site can be a source link in AI answers and can ground the response | Most businesses |
| Exclude my site's links and content | Not shown or linked in these features and not used to ground answers; no impressions or traffic from them | Publishers whose revenue comes directly from content |
| Inherit control from parent | The child property follows the closest parent with its own setting | Several subdomains or directories |

Changes usually apply within a few days; exclusion typically takes 1-2 days, and caching can make some
pages take longer. One more detail: if a child property chooses a different setting from its parent,
the owner of the domain property is notified.

## What the control affects, and what it does not

The most common confusion is treating this setting as a single master switch. In reality Google uses
your content along three separate paths, and each has its own switch.

<BlogFigure name="google-ai-search-controls" alt="Diagram of three separate paths: a single site on the left branches to three destinations on the right. The path to the classic results list at the top is always open; the path to the AI answer in the middle runs through a switch that can be turned off; the path to model training at the bottom is tied to a separate switch." caption="The Search Console setting only closes the middle path. Classic results are unaffected, and model training needs a separate choice." />

| Destination | Does the new control affect it | The actual tool |
|---|---|---|
| AI Overviews, AI Mode, Discover's AI features | Yes | Search Console → Search generative AI |
| Ranking in classic search results | No, it is not used as a signal | Regular SEO work |
| Training of AI models | No | Google-Extended |
| Leaving Search entirely | No | noindex |
| Merchant Center and Google Ads choices | No, it does not override them | Their own dashboards |

Google spells out two limits as well. Even when your site is excluded, **similar content from other
sites keeps appearing in AI answers**. An excluded site may also still be used for general Search
functions such as understanding query and page language.

## What the generative AI performance report shows

The [Generative AI performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139?hl=en)
that ships with the control opens as a separate tab under Performance. For the first time it answers
"do we appear in AI answers" with Google's own data rather than guesswork.

- **What it measures:** impressions, meaning how often a link to your site appeared in AI Overviews
  or AI Mode. If one answer contains several of your links, the chart counts it once.
- **Breakdowns:** page, country, date and device. The "Web: multimodal" filter separates impressions
  from Google Lens and image searches.
- **What is missing:** clicks, click-through rate, average position and query data. Google says more
  metrics will follow.
- **If you cannot see it:** your site may not have enough AI impressions yet, or it may be excluded
  from these features.

<Callout type="tip" title="Read the report side by side, not on its own">
  For the same pages, put AI impressions, clicks from the regular performance report and conversions
  from your analytics in one table. A page with high AI impressions and falling clicks is a page where
  the AI answer closes the question for you; it is missing a next step that brings the user to your site.
</Callout>

## Does opting out make sense: a decision guide

There is no single right answer, but the weight is clear by business type. Treat the table below as
a starting point, not a rule.

| Business type | Suggested setting | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Service company, B2B, corporate site | Stay included | Users are looking for a provider; being cited is visibility |
| Local business (clinic, restaurant, repair) | Stay included | Google says Business Profiles also help you appear in AI answers |
| E-commerce | Stay included | Product visibility also depends on Merchant Center; the setting does not override it |
| Guide-heavy content site, ad revenue | Decide with data | Revenue comes from the page being read; collect a few weeks of report data first |
| Paid content, subscriptions | Evaluate | The content itself is the product; summarising it may be a direct revenue loss |

<Callout type="warn" title="Opting out does not remove your competitors">
  Exclusion only affects your own site. Competing pages that answer the same question stay in the AI
  answer. For a business that sells services, that means the answer ends with their link instead of yours.
</Callout>

## What Google says about staying visible

Google's [generative AI search guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide),
published in May 2026, answers many of the "AI SEO" tips circulating in the market directly. The guide
says these features are built on Search's core ranking and quality systems, so the ground rule is still SEO.

- **What is required:** the page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet, the site must be
  included in these features, and you need crawlable content, semantic HTML, good page experience and
  original, first-hand content.
- **What is not required:** AI-specific text files, special markup or Markdown copies (Google Search
  ignores them, though other services may use them), splitting content into small chunks, rewriting
  "for AI", or collecting artificial brand mentions.
- **What to avoid:** mass-producing pages for query variations. Google treats this as a breach of its
  scaled content abuse spam policy.

Structured data is not required for these features but remains valuable for rich results. We covered
content structure in detail in our [SEO to GEO post](/en/blog/search-visibility-in-the-ai-era-seo-to-geo)
and the technical foundation in [how to build a fast website](/en/blog/how-to-build-a-fast-website).

## A 30-day action plan

1. **Check the setting.** In Search Console, go to Settings → Search generative AI, see which option is
   active and whether any child properties chose something different.
2. **Open the report and collect 4 weeks of data.** Export which pages appear in AI answers, in which
   countries and on which devices.
3. **Combine three sources.** Put AI impressions, regular search clicks and conversions in one table,
   page by page.
4. **Fix pages with high impressions and low clicks.** Move a next step the answer cannot close (a quote
   form, a calculator, a sample project, contact details) to the top of the page.
5. **Confirm eligibility.** Check that key pages are indexed, not blocked with nosnippet, and fast on mobile.
6. **Decide with data.** If you are considering exclusion, do it only after those 4 weeks of data and
   once it is clear whether your revenue comes from content or from customer demand.

## Conclusion

Google's new control, for the first time, leaves the decision to appear in AI answers to the site
owner. The data shows clicks fall noticeably on searches with an AI summary, but opting out keeps your
regular rankings while handing all visibility on that surface to competitors. For most service, local
and e-commerce sites, the right move is to stay included, use the new report to measure which pages
are used in AI answers, and strengthen those pages so they bring users to the site.
