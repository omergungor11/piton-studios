Türkiye's AI Action Plan, in force since August, sets out what the state will invest in and which
targets it will track in AI up to 2030. Most of the coverage focused on data centres, large language
models and the trillion-lira goal. For an SME the real question is closer to home: what does the plan
offer my business, and what should I do now? This post covers the parts of the plan that directly
concern SMEs, where businesses stand today according to TURKSTAT data, and the groundwork you can do
before support calls open. For a short summary of how businesses use AI, see our
[FAQ page](/en/faq#faq-ai-use-cases).

## What is the plan, and why does it matter now?

The plan came into force with [Presidential Circular No. 2026/9, published in the Official Gazette on 18 August 2026](https://www.alomaliye.com/2026/08/18/turkiye-yapay-zeka-eylem-plani-2026-2030/).
It follows the 2021-2025 National AI Strategy and is coordinated by the Ministry of Industry and
Technology. The circular lists raising AI awareness and training a skilled workforce among its core
goals.

[According to the details reported by Webrazzi](https://webrazzi.com/2026/08/18/turkiye-nin-2026-2030-yapay-zek-eylem-plani-yururluge-girdi/),
the plan is built around four axes called "Notice", "Use", "Produce" and "Govern", with four actions
in each, for 16 priority actions in total. The National AI Board will monitor implementation; progress
will be published on a public portal, with a first report within 12 months and annual reports after
that. In other words, how many targets are met will be trackable in the years ahead.

## The starting point: how much do businesses use AI?

The plan's targets only make sense against the starting point. According to TURKSTAT's
[AI Statistics 2026](https://bloomberght.com/turkiye-de-yapay-zeka-kullanimi-yaklasik-iki-kat-artti-3790100),
AI use among enterprises with 10 or more employees was 2.7% in 2021; it rose to 7.5% in 2025 and 14%
in 2026. The increase is fast, but the rate is still low: only one in seven enterprises uses any AI
technology.

It is also clear what the users do with it: marketing or sales (51%), research and development
(46.3%), and production or service processes (43.4%). 46.5% of these businesses have their solution
developed by external providers, while 32.1% develop it with their own staff.

## The gap between SMEs and large companies

Large companies pull the average up. In the same TURKSTAT data, 37.1% of enterprises with 250 or more
employees use AI, compared with 17% of those with 50-249 employees and 12.8% of those with 10-49.
Given this gap, it makes sense that the plan sets aside separate support for SMEs.

## The parts of the plan that directly concern SMEs

Most of the plan focuses on the public sector, infrastructure and research. These are the items that
can touch an SME's day-to-day work (as reported by
[Webrazzi](https://webrazzi.com/2026/08/18/turkiye-nin-2026-2030-yapay-zek-eylem-plani-yururluge-girdi/)
and [Teknoblog](https://www.teknoblog.com/turkiye-yapay-zeka-eylem-planini-acikladi/)):

| Item | Target | What it means for an SME |
|---|---|---|
| AI vouchers | At least 1,000 SMEs in the first 12 months; at least half of projects reach field pilots | Direct support for a first AI project trial |
| GPU for Everyone | At least 2 million GPU-hours a year, at least 20 million by end-2028 | Access to computing power to train or run models |
| AI literacy | 5 million people in two years, workshops in 81 provinces | A free resource for basic staff training |
| Applied specialists | 100,000 applied AI professionals by end-2027 | A larger pool of people to hire or work with |
| Open public data | At least 2,000 datasets, at least 100 via API | New data sources for products and analysis |
| Regulatory sandboxes | In at least 5 priority sectors | A controlled way to test new products in finance, health, energy, mobility and telecoms |

[According to Teknoblog](https://www.teknoblog.com/turkiye-yapay-zeka-eylem-planini-acikladi/), the
voucher support is aimed especially at technology access for SMEs in health, energy and smart
manufacturing. Another target is for at least a third of the supported pilots to become permanent
products or services, so support will go to projects that can deliver results.

<Callout type="info" title="Application conditions are not yet clear">
  The sources we reviewed do not include the voucher amounts, application timeline or eligibility
  conditions. These details will come with the relevant institutions' call announcements. Do not
  build a budget around a support amount before the call text is published.
</Callout>

## The real barrier: expertise

TURKSTAT data also shows why businesses that don't use AI stay away. The most common reason is lack of
relevant expertise (72.3%), followed by legal uncertainty (66.4%) and data protection and privacy
concerns (65.4%). 8.3% of enterprises don't use AI but are considering it.

The plan's literacy programme and specialist training targets address the first barrier, while
regulatory sandboxes and risk-based rules address the second. But results from these targets will take
years. In the short term, the way to close the expertise gap is to put someone who knows the business
process at the same table as someone who knows AI.

## Data and compliance

According to TURKSTAT, 16.6% of enterprises using AI process personal data with these technologies.
If customer messages, application forms or call recordings go into an AI tool, obligations under
Türkiye's data protection law (KVKK), such as privacy notices, cases that need explicit consent and
transfers abroad, should be part of the project from the start. For businesses serving Europe, the
[EU AI Act transparency rules](/en/blog/eu-ai-act-transparency-rules-chatbots-ai-content) also apply.

The plan foresees algorithmic impact assessments, model cards and security testing for high-impact AI
systems. The details of these rules have not been published yet, but recording which data is used for
which purpose is the cheapest preparation for any future compliance request.

<Callout type="warn" title="Do not upload customer data to free tools">
  Staff pasting customer information into general AI tools opened with personal accounts is the most
  common data risk in SMEs. Put in writing which tool can be used with which data, and prefer tools
  with a business contract and clear data processing terms.
</Callout>

## A preparation roadmap for SMEs

<BlogFigure name="turkiye-ai-action-plan" alt="The plan's four axes shown as four panels: a document and glowing node for awareness, a gauge and bars for use, a cube for production, and a shield for governance. Light beams from the four panels flow into a business panel in the centre; a rising line from the business panel reaches a growth chart on the right." caption="The plan's four axes (Notice, Use, Produce, Govern) reach the business as training, support, infrastructure and rules; what turns them into results is the business's own process and data preparation." />

The groundwork you can do before support calls open determines how much a voucher or grant is worth:

| Step | What to do | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Pick processes | List the 2-3 repetitive tasks that take the most time | A prioritised process list |
| 2. Measure | Record how many hours these tasks take today and their error rate | Baseline values |
| 3. Organise data | Map where the relevant data is, in what format and who holds it | A data inventory |
| 4. Compliance note | Write down whether personal data is involved and which tools can be used | A short internal rule |
| 5. Training | Send at least one team member to basic AI training | An internal owner |
| 6. Pilot plan | Design a small pilot for one process with a measurable target | An application-ready project summary |

For measurement and return calculations, our [post on AI automation and ROI for SMEs](/en/blog/ai-automation-roi-for-smbs)
offers a step-by-step method.

## Common mistakes

- **Postponing the project to wait for support.** Voucher amounts and timing are unknown; groundwork
  such as process selection and data organisation creates value regardless of support.
- **Choosing a tool and then looking for a problem.** Pick the AI tool after defining the job it needs
  to do.
- **Starting without measuring.** Without a baseline you cannot show whether a pilot worked, and the
  goal of turning pilots into permanent products depends on that.
- **Leaving compliance to the end.** In a project involving personal data, skipping the KVKK check at
  the start sends the project back before it goes live.
- **Relying on one person.** Concentrating AI knowledge in a single employee stalls the project when
  that person leaves.

## Conclusion

The AI Action Plan draws, for the first time, a support framework for SMEs with numerical targets:
vouchers, access to computing power and a broad training programme. The starting point is modest,
though; only 12.8% of small businesses use AI, and the biggest barrier is expertise. As the plan's
targets play out over the years, what will make the difference is a business being clear today about
what it expects from AI in which process.
